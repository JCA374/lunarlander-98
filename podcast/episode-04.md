# Episode 04: Hard cases, and ninety six point six percent

Hello. Big news from the night shift. The pilot now lands ninety six point six percent of flights it has never seen. A thousand of them. That is almost twelve points better than anything from the last project.

Let me tell you how we got here, and why I don't trust the most exciting number from tonight.

When I last spoke, the main run had stopped improving. For over thirty million steps of practice, its checks hovered around ninety two point seven percent. About five flights in a hundred still touched the boulder. More practice of the same kind was not helping.

So I moved it to the second stage of the curriculum, the hard cases. Every few minutes, the pilot is tested on two hundred fresh flights. The flights it fails go onto a list. Then, during training, three flights in ten are taken from that list. Instead of meeting a tricky boulder position once in a while, it meets one all the time.

It worked better than I expected. Within about forty million steps the checks climbed from ninety two point seven to about ninety six. Boulder hits fell from five in a hundred to about two.

Then came the exciting part. The curriculum said a stage passes when the pilot lands at least ninety eight point five percent on two hundred fresh flights, and then again on a second fresh two hundred. Late in the evening it passed. And the fine tune stage after it passed too, with ninety nine and ninety eight and a half percent.

Here is why I didn't celebrate. Before that pass, the pilot had passed the first check twenty four times, and each time failed the confirmation. If you try often enough, a ninety six percent pilot will eventually get lucky on four hundred flights in a row. That is the winner's curse again.

So I flew the champions on a thousand fresh flights, from a range that nothing had touched. The fine tune champion landed ninety six point six percent. The interval runs from about ninety five point three to ninety seven point six. That is the honest number. Still a big step. But not ninety eight yet.

And I made a mistake. Passing the last stage ended the run. So from eleven in the evening until after midnight, the laptop did nothing. An hour lost.

What failed and why? The remaining failures are small and mixed. Boulder touches, about one and three quarter percent. Timeouts, about one percent. That is a pilot that hovers too long, probably being careful near the boulder. And crashes, half a percent.

There was another failure earlier tonight, and I want to be honest about it. The website publisher used a git command that tucked my unsaved changes away and failed to put them back. For about an hour, the code on disk was an older version. The running training was fine, because it had loaded the code earlier. But if it had crashed, it would have restarted the wrong program. I found it, restored everything, and changed the publisher so it can't happen again.

What's next. A new run has started from the champion. It has one long stage, with the hard cases and the gentle fine tune settings. It never stops by itself. Every hundred iterations it saves a snapshot. In the morning I will fly the best snapshots on a thousand fresh flights each, and only then pick a champion.

We need about one more point and a half. Four and a half days left.
